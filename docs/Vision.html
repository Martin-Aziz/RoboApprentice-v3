Automation Passport is the bridge concept.

This is how “video-to-task” grows into “automation memory” without sounding like science fiction.

What is an Automation Passport?

It is like an ID card for a robot task. It stores what the human showed, what Aurevix understood, what robot/workcell was assumed, what code was created, what evidence was checked, who reviewed it, and whether it can be reused elsewhere.

Why does it matter commercially?

Because the customer is not only buying generated code. They are building a task library, approval history, and reusable automation memory. That creates trust, switching cost, and a reason to expand.

Why not say “Robot OS” now?

Because customers and investors need proof first. Start with one narrow, trusted task workflow. Once many passports exist, the bigger infrastructure story becomes natural.

What should the first design partner prove?

One real task should move from demonstration to task intent, simulation, generated code, evidence checklist, qualified human review, and exportable task record. That is the proof that Aurevix is not only a beautiful demo.

Three reusable talk tracks.

Use these in co-founder alignment, customer calls, and investor conversations.

For your co-founder

We are not changing direction. We are sequencing the vision. First, we help factories teach one robot task from video. Then every approved task becomes a reusable record. Over time, those records become the factory’s automation memory.

Best use: internal alignment.

For customers

Show the task once. Aurevix turns it into a robot-specific task plan, simulation, controller code, and evidence package. Your team reviews it before anything moves, then saves it for reuse later.

Best use: website and demo calls.

For investors

The wedge is video-based robot retasking. The larger company is the neutral automation memory layer for factories: every task taught, simulated, reviewed, versioned, approved, reused, and eventually deployed across robot brands.

Best use: pitch narrative.

Next design partner call: five questions.

Keep the meeting narrow. The goal is not to show the whole platform. The goal is to prove one valuable task through the trust loop.

What exact task should we test?

Pick one constrained, real, repeatable task. Avoid broad demos.

What robot and workcell are involved?

Robot, gripper, fixture, safety setup, payload, and constraints.

What is the baseline today?

How long, how expensive, and how annoying is retasking now?

Who needs to review it?

Engineer, safety owner, integrator, or operations lead.

What evidence proves value?

Define what makes them say: “This is useful enough to continue.”

The sentence to keep repeating

Aurevix starts by turning a demonstrated task into a trusted, reviewable, reusable automation record. If we own enough of those records, we become the automation memory layer for factories.
